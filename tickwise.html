Timing trace

Each mark is one beat, drawn against where the target rate says it should land. Circles and squares are alternate beats. Rising lines mean slow, falling lines mean fast, and the gap between the two lines is the beat error. Faint crosses are ticks ignored as stray sounds.

Rate history

Seconds per day against target, each point averaged over the window in settings. The shaded band is the 95% range. Watch it settle and narrow after each adjustment.

Sound level

The last two seconds after filtering. Triangles mark detected ticks and the dashed line is the detection threshold.

Settings and calibration

A tick must be this many times louder than the background to count. Raise it to ignore more noise, lower it to catch fainter ticks.

Removes room rumble and voices so only the sharp tick remains.

Longer is steadier but reacts more slowly to an adjustment.

Record a quartz clock that ticks once a second (3,600 bph) for a couple of minutes, then press Calibrate. Your device's clock is then corrected to match it.

Every timing comes from your device's audio clock, which is typically 10 to 50 ppm off (1 to 4 s/day) until calibrated. The 95% range covers tick-timing scatter only and assumes timing errors are independent. Ticks that do not fit the steady beat are ignored, and the range widens when many are.
